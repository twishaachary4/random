How a Meridian gets made

Every case starts as a solid titanium blank and ends, eleven days later, on a watchmaker's bench in the Jura mountains. Nothing here is automated for the sake of speed.

  1. 01

    Machining

    The case is turned from a single titanium billet, leaving no seams for moisture or dust to find.

  2. 02

    Finishing

    Each surface is hand-brushed or polished in sequence, so light moves across the case the way it should.

  3. 03

    Regulation

    The movement is timed across six positions until it holds within four seconds a day.

  4. 04

    Inspection

    Pressure-tested to 100 metres, then worn for 48 hours before it's allowed to ship.
